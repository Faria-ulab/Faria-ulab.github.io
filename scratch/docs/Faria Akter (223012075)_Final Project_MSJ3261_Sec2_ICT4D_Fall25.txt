Introduction: ICT for Development (ICT4D) involves utilizing Information and  Communication Technology to enhance social, economic, and political progress, particularly in  underdeveloped nations. The initiative harnesses resources like computers, mobile devices, the  internet, and digital platforms to elevate living standards and empower communities. ICT4D  gives individuals access to essential information regarding health, education, agriculture, and  market trends, which aids them in making educated choices and tackling difficulties. It also  promotes remote education, telehealth services, e-governance, and digital finance, thereby  improving the accessibility and efficiency of crucial services. By addressing the digital divide,  ICT4D promotes social inclusion, empowers disadvantaged groups, and fosters engagement in  development activities. Additionally, it promotes economic growth by supporting  entrepreneurship, creating jobs, and expanding access to broader markets. In summary, ICT4D  leverages technology to promote human advancement, social empowerment, and sustainable  development. In Bangladesh, ICT for Development plays a vital role in enhancing education,  healthcare, and governance. Efforts such as digital classrooms, e-health services, and e government platforms increase access to essential services, empower local communities, and  contribute to economic growth. ICT4D helps to close the digital gap, promoting social inclusion  and fostering sustainable development throughout the nation. For my project, I chose the “Hasina  and Friends” website. "Hasina and Friends" is the first interactive online learning platform  launched by the government of Bangladesh, designed for children aged 6 to 16. It features games  and stories that help them explore the country's history, culture, environment, health, and digital  literacy. But sadly, this innovative project stopped after 2 years. This project had a budget of 180  million TK. This is what makes this incredible project a failure within days. 
This analysis aims to investigate the “Hasina and Friends” ICT project to uncover the reasons for  its failure. The study intends to identify the technical, organizational, and social obstacles that  impeded its success, as well as to derive insights for future ICT4D initiatives in Bangladesh. The  focus will be on the project's design, execution, stakeholder participation, and overall social  impact. Additionally, it will take into account external elements such as funding, policy backing,  and community involvement. Any projects unrelated to “Hasina and Friends” or the wider  ICT4D landscape in Bangladesh will not be included in this examination.
Project Overview: The "Hasina and Friends" project was an ICT initiative initiated by the ICT  Division of the Government of Bangladesh as part of the larger Innovation Design and  Entrepreneurship Academy (IDEA) project. This web-based interactive platform aimed to  educate children and young people through engaging digital stories and games. The project  received significant government funding, with about BDT 18–20 crore specifically allocated for  the website component from the overall IDEA budget of BDT 443 crore. However, detailed  information regarding the total funding and duration of the project is not widely available in  public records. The "Hasina and Friends" portal was officially launched on September 28, 2022,  coinciding with Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s birthday, and was intended to function as a  lasting digital educational resource thereafter.The goals of the "Hasina and Friends" platform  were to create an engaging and interactive learning space for children aged 6 to 16, focusing on  topics such as the environment, health, education, culture, national values, and digital  connectivity through stories and games. The site featured 32 interactive stories and 12 games  designed for various age groups, aiming to both entertain and educate users on important themes.  It aimed to foster creativity, patriotism, and responsible citizenship among the youth of  Bangladesh in a digital context. The main audience for the project was children and teenagers  aged 6 to 16, divided into three age groups (6–9, 9–12, and 12–16), with the content tailored to  meet age-specific learning goals. The initiative sought to engage young learners throughout  Bangladesh, providing a localized digital platform available in both Bengali and English to  enhance accessibility. The platform utilized a web portal, interactive gaming applications, and  digital storytelling techniques to engage its users. While specific details regarding the backend  infrastructure or software frameworks were not publicly disclosed, the project integrated web  development and game design to produce an educational technology resource designed for  internet-connected devices. 
The project's alignment with national digital objectives can be understood within the framework  of Bangladesh's Digital Bangladesh Vision 2021. This initiative emphasizes the use of ICT to  enhance education, expand digital accessibility, and foster the development of a knowledge based society. "Hasina and Friends" was positioned within this broader strategy by offering an  innovative digital learning tool aimed at cultivating digital literacy and knowledge among the  youth, in line with the government's goal of integrating ICT into education and youth  involvement.
Implementation Process: The implementation of the Hasina and Friends platform commenced  with its planning and tendering phase under the Innovation Design and Entrepreneurship  Academy (IDEA) project by the ICT Division of Bangladesh. Initial steps included project  conceptualization, setting objectives, and budget allocation, with an estimated allocation of BDT  18–20 crore for development. In March 2021, the ICT Division issued a tender for the creation of  12 gaming applications as part of the project, followed by another tender in July 2021 for the  web portal, indicating a multi-phase procurement method to engage developers and vendors. 
Once the tenders were awarded, the design and development phase began, with contracted IT  firms working on the interactive stories, educational games, and web interface tailored for  children aged 6 to 16. The platform was developed in both Bengali and English, featuring 32  stories and 12 games intended to educate users on topics such as the environment, health,  education, and digital connectivity. 
The launch concluded with a prominent inauguration on September 28, 2022, coinciding with  Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s birthday. This ceremony was attended by government officials  and stakeholders, marking the completion of the platform’s development. 
Regarding management structures, the ICT Division was responsible for coordination, with  designated project directors and government officials overseeing supervision and reporting.  Budget allocations were made for software development, content production, and promotional  activities. However, the management and coordination strategies faced criticism due to post implementation audits revealing cost irregularities and questioning the effectiveness of the  planning and monitoring processes. By early 2025, the platform was taken offline, indicating  issues with sustainability and operational management. 
In summary, while the project adhered to standard stages from planning and tendering to  development and launch, the absence of sustained operational support and oversight diminished  its long-term viability.  
Analysis of Failure: The Hasina and Friends project is often viewed as an unsuccessful ICT4D  initiative due to major flaws in its planning, execution, governance, and long-term viability. A  critical issue was the inadequate planning and suboptimal project design. While the platform 
intended to deliver digital learning resources for children, there is scant evidence of conducting a  thorough needs assessment or pilot testing prior to its widespread launch. Reports suggest that  around BDT 18-20 crore was allocated for the development of the web portal and games, an  amount significantly higher than the typical market rates for comparable educational platforms.  This indicates a lack of effective cost-benefit analysis and poor resource management, which are  frequently cited as reasons for the failure of large-scale government ICT projects (Prothom Alo,  2024). In addition to issues related to financial inefficiency, the project faced significant  technical and infrastructural challenges. Although Bangladesh has made strides in digital  accessibility, consistent internet connectivity and device availability for children, particularly in  rural regions, remain inconsistent. The project depended primarily on a web-based platform that  lacked sufficient offline or low-bandwidth options, which limited its outreach to the very  demographic it aimed to assist. Furthermore, just two years after its launch, the platform became  inaccessible, highlighting inadequate technical support and the lack of a sustainable operational  strategy (Prothom Alo, 2023). Capacity deficiencies and inadequate management frameworks  further weakened the initiative. After the project's implementation, investigations indicated that  officials in charge had insufficient knowledge regarding budget details and technical  deliverables, highlighting a limited institutional capacity to manage complex ICT systems  effectively. This deficiency in skilled monitoring and evaluation diminished accountability,  allowing inefficiencies to remain unaddressed. 
Additionally, political and administrative factors influenced the project. The branding, launch  timing, and symbolic context of the initiative suggest that political visibility may have been  prioritized over its educational effectiveness. This politicization shifted attention away from  essential aspects like pedagogical quality, user engagement, and long-term impact, a trend noted  in various state-led ICT4D projects (Prothom Alo, 2024). Ultimately, the project faltered due to  insufficient stakeholder involvement and a lack of long-term sustainability planning. Key  participants such as teachers, parents, education specialists, and children were not adequately  engaged in the design of the content or in providing feedback. As the initial funding and  government interest waned, there was no system in place for updating content, fostering  community ownership, or integrating the platform within the education system. As a result, the  platform quickly became irrelevant and lost the support it needed to operate.
In summary, the failure of "Hasina and Friends" underscores the risks associated with ICT4D  initiatives when there isn't a balance between technological aspirations and thoughtful planning,  institutional capacity, accountability, and sustainable design. 
Impacts and Consequences: The abrupt cessation of the Hasina and Friends project had notable  social, institutional, and policy implications. For the intended audience, particularly young  learners, the termination meant the loss of a potentially enriching digital educational tool. Many  children became aware of the platform through promotional activities and launch events, but its  unexpected unavailability led to confusion and diminished trust in digital learning initiatives.  This adversely impacted attempts to foster consistent digital engagement among children,  especially at a time when ICT-driven education was being increasingly prioritized across the  country. The failure of the project at the institutional level significantly damaged the credibility  of the ICT Division and other involved organizations. Public reports pointing to overspending,  inadequate oversight, and a lack of transparency raised substantial concerns about governance  and accountability in government-led ICT4D efforts. Consequently, trust among policymakers,  development practitioners, and the general public in large-scale ICT investments diminished.  This skepticism could deter future funding approvals or collaboration from stakeholders for  similar digital education initiatives. The project led to several unintended negative outcomes,  such as reinforcing the perception that some ICT initiatives prioritize political visibility over  genuine developmental impact, fostering public skepticism towards state-sponsored digital  programs. Significant financial and human resources were wasted on a project that failed to  produce lasting results, representing a lost opportunity to support more affordable, community driven digital learning initiatives. Ultimately, this failure demonstrates how poorly planned ICT4D projects can damage public trust and slow progress toward inclusive digital development. 
Lessons Learned and Proposed Recommendations: The failure of the Hasina and Friends  project offers several crucial insights for the design and implementation of ICT4D initiatives in  Bangladesh. One significant takeaway is that technology-focused projects cannot thrive without  a solid development rationale. Despite its digital aspirations, the project suffered from a lack of  adequate needs assessment and genuine stakeholder involvement, leading to poor relevance and 
minimal long-term effects (Prothom Alo, 2023). This highlights a prevalent issue in ICT4D,  where initiatives are often created in a top-down fashion, overlooking users' contexts and daily  realities (Heeks, 2002). Another important lesson relates to financial planning and accountability.  Reports of overspending without corresponding results suggest inadequate cost-benefit analysis  and ineffective monitoring, which ultimately eroded public confidence (Prothom Alo, 2024). The  project also reveals gaps in institutional capacity. Limited technical expertise among officials  hindered effective vendor oversight and compromised quality control. Research indicates that  weak institutional capacity can lead to project failures, even with adequate funding and political  backing (Heeks, 2017). Additionally, the lack of a sustainability strategy, such as ongoing  funding, content updates, or maintenance, illustrates how many government ICT initiatives falter  once initial political or administrative interest wanes (Prothom Alo, 2023). Based on these  insights, several actionable recommendations can be made. Firstly, future ICT projects should  adopt a user-centered design approach, engaging children, teachers, parents, and education  experts from the outset to enhance relevance and usability. Secondly, strong monitoring and  evaluation frameworks must be established, incorporating independent audits and measurable  performance indicators for transparency and efficiency. Thirdly, capacity building within  government agencies is crucial; project managers should receive training in ICT project  management and impact assessment. 
Lastly, sustainability should be integrated into project design through long-term operational  planning, dedicated maintenance budgets, and alignment with existing education systems.  Implementing these strategies can help Bangladesh avoid repeating past ICT4D failures and  ensure that future initiatives lead to inclusive and sustainable development. 
Conclusion: The evaluation of the Hasina and Friends initiative highlights how even well intentioned ICT projects can falter when technological ambitions lack robust planning,  governance, and sustainability measures. While the project aimed to enhance digital learning for  children and was in line with national digital development objectives, it was plagued by poor  planning, inflated expenses, limited engagement from stakeholders, and insufficient institutional  capacity. Additionally, technical shortcomings, the absence of long-term maintenance plans, and  a lack of user-focused design further diminished its effectiveness, leading to its premature 
termination. Consequently, despite considerable public funding, the project failed to achieve  significant or lasting developmental impacts. This case provides valuable insights into the  challenges of digital development in Bangladesh. It reveals that top-down, politically motivated  ICT projects often focus more on visibility than on actual impact, which diminishes public trust  and wastes resources. The failure of "Hasina and Friends" emphasizes the importance of  participatory planning, accountability, and capacity building in ICT4D efforts. Ultimately, it  highlights the need for digital development in Bangladesh to prioritize inclusive, transparent, and  sustainable solutions that truly meet user needs and address development goals.
